With eleven minutes left in Lisbon, Spain were two goals down and the stadium had started to sing the home songs. Forty minutes later, Lucía Ortega walked to the spot for the fifth penalty, waited for the whistle and put the ball in the top corner.

It was the kind of final that nobody in the Spanish camp will describe the same way twice. Elena Garmendia, the coach, called it "the worst eighty minutes and the best half hour" of her time in charge.

## Eighty minutes of nothing

For most of the night Spain looked tired. The passing that carried them through the group was slow, the full backs stayed deep and the hosts scored twice from the same move down the left, once before half time and once just after.

Garmendia made three changes on the hour. None of them worked straight away. "I was not thinking about the trophy any more," she said afterwards. "I was thinking about how to lose with some dignity."

> We stopped playing the way we planned and started playing the way we play in the park. That is when it turned.

## Eleven minutes

The first goal came from a corner that should never have been given. Marina Prats met it at the near post and the ball went in off the goalkeeper's shoulder. Four minutes later Ainhoa Etxeberria ran from the halfway line, past two players, and finished low with her weaker foot.

Extra time was nervous and almost silent. Both sides had chances, neither took them, and by the end most of the players were walking.

## The shoot-out

Spain went first and scored their first four. The hosts missed their third. Ortega, who had only come on in the 110th minute, asked to take the last one.

"She told me in the huddle," Garmendia said. "I did not argue. When a player asks for that one, you let her."

## What happens next

The squad flies home on Thursday. The league restarts at the weekend, and nine of the starting eleven play for clubs with games on Saturday. Qualification for the next World Cup begins in February.
